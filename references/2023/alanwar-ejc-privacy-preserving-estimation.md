The paper behind CORA's `intersectStrip` for constrained zonotopes (`@conZonotope/intersectStrip.m` cites it) and the `CZN-B` observer of `linearSysDT`. It runs a set-based observer on an untrusted aggregator over Paillier-encrypted zonotope centers and strip positions. Its four lemmas are the plaintext set arithmetic the aggregator does, and they are formalized: the intersection of a zonotope or a constrained zonotope with a family of strips (`Lemma 1`, `Lemma 2`), of several zonotopes with weights (`Lemma 3`) and of several constrained zonotopes at once (`Lemma 4`). The rest is cryptography: the cryptosystem, the notions of computational indistinguishability and the four privacy theorems about the two protocols, which CORALean does not cover (it has no model of parties, keys, ciphertexts or probabilistic polynomial-time adversaries).

## Findings

**`Lemma 2` needs the radii nonnegative.** The strip `{x \| \|H x − y\| ≤ r}` is empty for `r < 0`, and the lemma's constrained zonotope is then not: the new factor ν_jk ∈ [−1, 1] of the row `H_j Ĝ ξ − r_j ν_jk = y_jk − (H_j ĉ)_k` reaches exactly the residues `(H_j x − y_j)_k = r_j ν_jk` of absolute value at most `|r_j|`, so for `r_j < 0` the lemma's set is the intersection with the strip of radius `|r_j|`, which is not empty. The paper calls r the bound on the measurement noise (`R_k = ⟨0, diag(r)⟩`, a zonotope of radii) and never states `r ≥ 0`. `ConZonotope.intersectStripFamily_exact` carries `∀ j, 0 ≤ r j` as a hypothesis and holds at every gain `λ_j`, so the lemma is proved as printed up to that. The same lemma for the zonotopic case, `Lemma 1`, needs no such hypothesis: an empty strip is contained in anything.
