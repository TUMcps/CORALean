**Source:** http://congres.cran.univ-lorraine.fr/2003/CDC%202003/pdffiles/papers/FrP07.2.pdf (the authors' six-page conference version, "Guaranteed state estimation by zonotopes", 42nd IEEE Conference on Decision and Control, Maui, 2003, paper FrP07.2, doi:10.1109/CDC.2003.1271935; read in full, text extracted with `pypdf`). The Automatica article (doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2004.12.008) is closed access and no copy was reachable (Unpaywall lists none), so **the rows below are the conference version's results under its own numbering, not the journal's**.  
**Inventory:** unconfirmed (against the journal article: the conference version prints Definitions 1-11, Theorems 1-5, Properties 1-2 and Algorithm 1, each with its own counter, and the volume formula of Sec. 6.1 without a number; the article is longer, CORA citing from it a gain that minimizes the F-radius and calling the volume formula "the last equation before Sec. 7", so its numbering and its further results are not known)

The origin of the strip intersection and of the zonotope inclusion behind CORA's strip-based
estimators, cited by [Le et al. 2013](../2013/le-automatica-zonotopic-estimation.md) for its
`Prop. 4` and `Prop. 6`, which are `Thm. 4` and `Property 2` here. Guaranteed state estimation for
a nonlinear discrete-time system: bound the next state set by an inclusion function (a mean-value
zonotope, `Thm. 5`), bound the states consistent with each measured component by a strip
(`Property 1`), and bound the zonotope-strip intersection by a zonotope in a gain `λ`
(`Property 2`), chosen to minimize the volume, a convex problem (`Sec. 6.1`). The volume formula
and the zonotope inclusion are what CORA's `volume_` and `intersectStrip` cite this paper for. The
F-radius gain CORA calls `'alamo-FRad'`, `λ = G Gᵀ c / (cᵀ G Gᵀ c + σ²)`, is not in the conference
version and so has no row; for one strip it is the Frobenius-norm minimizer the tree proves for
[Alanwar et al. 2023](../2023/alanwar-jfi-diffusion-observers.md) (`stripGain_optimal`). Misprints
and one inconsistency in the definition of the order are under Findings; no numbered result is false.

## Findings

**The rows are the conference version's.** The journal article of the same title and authors (Automatica 41(6), 2005) could not be obtained, so the inventory is that of its 2003 conference precursor, marked unconfirmed. Where the article renumbers, a row's label is wrong; the results and their proofs are the conference version's. The tree cites the paper as "Alamo et al. 2005" for the strip bound (`Property 2`) and the zonotope inclusion (`Thm. 4`), both of which the conference version has in the same form.

**Def. 11 — the order of a zonotope.** A zonotope of order `m` is defined as the Minkowski sum of `m` parallelepipeds, each a linear image `M Bq` of a unit box under a square `M`, which has `n` generators; the order is then `m` parallelepipeds and `m n` segments. `Thm. 4`, `Thm. 5` and `Property 2` write the zonotope `p ⊕ M Bᵐ` and `p ⊕ H Bʳ` and call `m` (and `r`) its order, counting segments, and `Thm. 5` states `l = 2n + m` generators for `q ⊕ S Bⁿ ⊕ ⋄M Bᵐ`, which counts segments too (`n + n + m`). The formalization takes the second reading throughout (`Zonotope` has a generator count); the definition as printed is a different count of the same sets.

**Misprints in the statements.** `Def. 1` prints the measurement equation as `y_i = g(x_j, v_j)`, the index `i` of the output component where the time `j` is meant. `Property 1` introduces "vector `c_i` and scalars `d_i, σ_i`" and then uses `s_i`; the proof and the strip use `s_i`. Neither affects a result.

**`Thm. 5` does not state that `f` is differentiable.** Its proof is the mean value extension of `Thm. 3`, which needs `f` differentiable in `x` along the segment from `p` to each point of `X`, and the interval matrix `□∇ₓf(X, W) H` presupposes the gradient. The formalization assumes differentiability in `x` for each `w ∈ W`; the natural interval extensions that give `q ⊕ S Bⁿ` and `M` are hypotheses, since computing them is `Thm. 2`'s business. The mean value theorem is applied once per coordinate, at a point `ξ_i` of the segment that depends on `i`; the `ξ_i` differ, and the rows `∇ₓf(ξ_i, w) H` are assembled into one matrix of the interval matrix, which is entrywise, so the argument stands. The paper's `f(x, w) ∈ f(p, w) ⊕ ∇ₓf(X, w) H Bᵐ` is read that way.

**`Sec. 6.1` is proved for the sum formula.** The volume of a zonotope as a sum of `2ⁿ |det|` over the `n`-subsets of its generators is quoted as well known and is CORA's `volume_` at `'exact'` (`Zonotope.volume`); that it is the Lebesgue measure is proved where the zonotope has `n` generators and not for more. `Thm. 1` of the later paper by [Bravo et al. 2006](../2006/bravo-tac-bounded-error-identification.md) repeats the derivation with the same formula; the identity proved here is between `Zonotope.volume` of the strip intersection and the printed expression, and the convexity of `λ ↦ Vol` is of that expression.
