# Using Four-Valued Signal Temporal Logic for Incremental Verification of Hybrid Systems

F. Lercher and M. Althoff. "Using Four-Valued Signal Temporal Logic for Incremental Verification of Hybrid Systems". CAV, LNCS 14683, pp. 259-281, 2024.

**Source:** https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-031-65633-0_12.pdf (the published chapter, open access under CC BY, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-65633-0_12, 23 pages; read in full apart from the bibliography, text extracted with PyMuPDF; ar5iv was not used). Numbering is the printed one.  
**Inventory:** confirmed (Lemmas 1-3, Theorems 1-2, Corollary 1, Algorithms 1-2, Remark 1 and the two numbered displays, Eqs. (1) and (2); the paper has no numbered definition and no appendix, so the definitions and the claims stated in prose without a number are rows of their own, named by section)

CORA's `onlineReachSetAnalyzer` checks an STL formula on a reachable set while the reachability analysis runs, and stops it as soon as the verdict is conclusive. Its values are the four of CORA's `fourValued` (`False`, `Unknown` = ⊣₁, `Inconclusive` = ⊣₂, `True`), extending Kleene's `kleene`; its signals are `pointSegmentSignal`, `kleeneSignal` and `fourValuedSignal`, its time intervals `stlInterval`. A satisfaction signal is built from the Boolean signal operators by taking the operation on the under- and the overapproximation of an uncertain signal and joining the two (`Sec. 4.2`, `Sec. 4.3`), and updated by propagating new observations up the syntax tree (`Alg. 2`).

CORALean formalizes the signal classes this rests on and everything they say about the paper's operators: the until of Boolean signals (`Eq. 1`, `Lemma 2`), the three- and four-valued operators through refinements (`Sec. 4.2`, `Sec. 4.3`), the direct semantics of the three-valued ones (`Eq. 2`), and the merge and conclusive interval of `Alg. 2`. Not formalized: STL over executions and reachable sets, and with it `Thm. 1`, `Thm. 2`, `Cor. 1` and `Alg. 1`.

Reading it found that the signals of the paper are not restricted to finite variability where `Lemma 2` and the theorems need it, and that the last sentence of `Cor. 1` fails for a system without executions, each below.

## Findings

**`Sec. 2.3` and `Lemma 2` need finite variability, and the paper asks for it of the predicate signals only.** `Sec. 2.3` says every Boolean signal is a disjunction of unitary signals and `Lemma 2` defines `(λ₁ U_I λ₂)(t)` by the finite disjunction over `n₁`, `n₂` of the minimal decomposition. A Boolean signal with `λ(t) = ⊤` exactly at `t = 1/n`, `n ≥ 1`, changes infinitely often in `[0,1]`; its unitary components are contiguous intervals, so the points `{1/n}` are one component each, and there is no finite decomposition: `n₁` is not a number, and the operator is undefined. `Sec. 2.5` excludes this for the signals `a∘ξ` of executions, but `Lemma 2` is stated for all Boolean signals, and so are `Thm. 1` and `Thm. 2`, for an arbitrary `R : ℝ≥0 → 2^X`: `â(R(t))` of an atomic predicate changes infinitely often for `R(t)` alternating between a set inside `⟦a⟧` and one meeting both sides at every `1/n`, and then the `U₁`-operators, which go through the Boolean ones, are undefined. `Sec. 5.1` notes that the signals of `Alg. 1` have finite variability if the reach sequence is free of Zeno behavior; the statements need that hypothesis. The Lean signals have finitely many pieces (`PointSegmentSignal.IsValid`), which supplies it for `Lemma 2` (`PointSegmentSignal.until_spec`), `Eq. 2` and `Sec. 4.3`.

**`Cor. 1`'s last sentence fails for a system without executions.** "`⟦φ⟧_R̃(0) = ⊥` implies `H ⊭ φ`" is argued from the covered executions all violating `φ`; `H ⊭ φ` needs one of them. For `φ = ¬true` and every `R̃` the value is `⟦¬true⟧_R̃(0) = ¬⊤ = ⊥`, while a hybrid system `H` with no initial state has no execution, so `H ⊨ ¬true` holds vacuously by the definition of `Sec. 2.5`. The statement holds for systems with at least one execution (of finite variability); `Cor. 1` is `—`, so this is read off the printed argument and has no Lean counterexample.
