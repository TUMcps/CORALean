(1) and (2); the paper has no numbered definition and no appendix, so the definitions and the claims stated in prose without a number are rows of their own, named by section)

CORA's `onlineReachSetAnalyzer` checks an STL formula on a reachable set while the reachability analysis runs, and stops it as soon as the verdict is conclusive. Its values are the four of CORA's `fourValued` (`False`, `Unknown` = ⊣₁, `Inconclusive` = ⊣₂, `True`), extending Kleene's `kleene`; its signals are `pointSegmentSignal`, `kleeneSignal` and `fourValuedSignal`, its time intervals `stlInterval`. A satisfaction signal is built from the Boolean signal operators by taking the operation on the under- and the overapproximation of an uncertain signal and joining the two (`Sec. 4.2`, `Sec. 4.3`), and updated by propagating new observations up the syntax tree (`Alg. 2`).

CORALean formalizes the signal classes this rests on and everything they say about the paper's operators. They are the truth order on the four values (`Sec. 2.2 (order)`), the until of unitary and of Boolean signals (`Eq. 1`, `Lemma 1`, `Lemma 2`) and the minimal unitary decomposition, the three- and four-valued operators through refinements (`Sec. 4.2`, `Sec. 4.3`, `Fig. 3`) and their monotonicity (`Lemma 3`), the direct semantics of the three-valued ones (`Eq. 2`), and the merge and conclusive interval of `Alg. 2`. STL over executions with the strict until is `FourValuedSTL`, a development of its own beside `STL.Formula`, which has the until of Roehm et al.: the satisfaction signals over reachable sets and partial reachable sets are built from the signal operators, and `Thm. 1`, `Thm. 2` and `Cor. 1` are proved for them. `Alg. 1` with the propagation of `Alg. 2` is proved for CORA's analyzer: after observations at new times the root signal is the satisfaction signal of `Sec. 4.3`, and a conclusive verdict is never revised. Not formalized: the branching of `Sec. 5.2`.

Reading it found that the signals of the paper are not restricted to finite variability where `Lemma 2` and the theorems need it, and that the last sentence of `Cor. 1` fails for a system without executions, each below.

## Findings

**`Sec. 2.3` and `Lemma 2` need finite variability, and the paper asks for it of the predicate signals only.** `Sec. 2.3` says every Boolean signal is a disjunction of unitary signals and `Lemma 2` defines `(λ₁ U_I λ₂)(t)` by the finite disjunction over `n₁`, `n₂` of the minimal decomposition. A Boolean signal with `λ(t) = ⊤` exactly at `t = 1/n`, `n ≥ 1`, changes infinitely often in `[0,1]`; its unitary components are contiguous intervals, so the points `{1/n}` are one component each, and there is no finite decomposition: `n₁` is not a number, and the operator is undefined. `Sec. 2.5` excludes this for the signals `a∘ξ` of executions, but `Lemma 2` is stated for all Boolean signals, and so are `Thm. 1` and `Thm. 2`, for an arbitrary `R : ℝ≥0 → 2^X`. There `â(R(t))` of an atomic predicate changes infinitely often for `R(t)` alternating between a set inside `⟦a⟧` and one meeting both sides at every `1/n`. Then the `U₁`-operators, which go through the Boolean ones, are undefined. `Sec. 5.1` notes that the signals of `Alg. 1` have finite variability if the reach sequence is free of Zeno behavior; the statements need that hypothesis. The Lean signals have finitely many pieces (`PointSegmentSignal.IsValid`), which supplies it for `Lemma 2` (`PointSegmentSignal.until_spec`), `Eq. 2` and `Sec. 4.3`, and for the satisfaction signals of `Thm. 1`, `Thm. 2` and `Cor. 1`, whose atomic signals `t ↦ â(R(t))` are required valid (`FourValuedSTL.IsAtomSignal`, `IsPartialAtomSignal`). The unitary decomposition (`PointSegmentSignal.exists_minimal_unitary_decomposition`) and the until are theorems about them, and the satisfaction signals are built from them by operators that exist for no others.

**`Cor. 1`'s last sentence fails for a system without executions.** "`⟦φ⟧_R̃(0) = ⊥` implies `H ⊭ φ`" is argued from the covered executions all violating `φ`; `H ⊭ φ` needs one of them. For `φ = ¬true` and every `R̃` the value is `⟦¬true⟧_R̃(0) = ¬⊤ = ⊥`, while a hybrid system `H` with no initial state has no execution, so `H ⊨ ¬true` holds vacuously by the definition of `Sec. 2.5`. The statement holds for systems with at least one execution (of finite variability), which `FourValuedSTL.verdict_system` proves; `FourValuedSTL.not_holdsOn_of_ff_counterexample` is the system above in Lean, with `φ = ¬true` over `Unit` and no executions. A system with no execution is the empty case, as the empty set is for a set operation, so `Cor. 1` is `underspecified` and not `fixed` (references/README.md, "The `Class` column"): the hypothesis `E.Nonempty` is the one missing.

**`Alg. 1` needs each time observed once.** The loop sets `ap.signal` on the interval `I` of each new reachable set, and the correctness of the propagation rests on the times of `I` being unknown before. `Alg. 2` drops what a parent has decided: the children's signals are overwritten with ⊣₂ on their irrelevant prefix, and `Merge` keeps the parent's value wherever the new `Δ` is ⊣₂. If a later observation sets a time again, with another value, the parent cannot see it. The paper's partial reachable set `R̃ : T → 2^X` has one set per time, which excludes this, but `Alg. 1` does not say the intervals of `reach.NextStep()` are disjoint. Closed time intervals of consecutive steps share an endpoint. `OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.observeSet_correct` assumes the observation intervals pairwise disjoint and nonempty.

**The counterexample.** `OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.observeSet_correct_counterexample` drops the disjointness, over the boxes of `ℝ` and the atom `x₀ ∈ [-10, 0]`, with the analyzer propagating down. For `φ = F_[0,2] p`, observing `p` false on `[0,2]` makes the root `⊥` at `0` and inconclusive after it, so only time `0` of `p` is erased. A second observation with `p` unknown on `[1,2]` starts after the erased prefix. `Δ` is ⊣₂ at `0`, since `p(0)` is erased, so the root keeps `⊥`, while the satisfaction signal of the latest atom values is ⊣₁ at `0`.

**The paper's own run is refused by CORA.** For `φ = F_[0,2] p`, observing `p = ⊤` on `[1,2]` makes the root `⊤` on `[0,2]` and erases `p` there. Observing `p = ⊥` on `[1,2]` would give `Δ = ⊣₂` at time `0` (`p` is unknown after `2`), so the root would keep `⊤`. In CORA's `observeSet` the first step records `[0,2]` as propagated into `p`, and an observation from `1` on is refused (`OnlineAnalyzerNode.conflicts`, CORA's `specialError`). It refuses only an observation that starts inside the propagated prefix, and the prefix ends where the root stops being conclusive; that is why the Lean instance has the prefix `{0}`. This is read off the code, not proved in Lean.

**`Alg. 2` — the merge and the conclusive interval.** `FourValuedSignal.merge_spec` (line 9),
`FourValuedSignal.conclusiveInterval_spec` (line 11: `t` is in the interval iff `t ≥ 0` and the
signal is conclusive or ignored at every time up to `t`). In the counterexample of row `Alg. 1`, the child
erased by line 11 is read again by the next `Merge` as ⊣₂, so the root keeps its value.

**`Alg. 1` — the atoms, the final verdict and the structure.** Two atoms of `φ` with one label must
be one atom, since CORA's cache tells atoms apart by their labels.
`OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.observeSet_verdict_final`: a conclusive verdict (not ⊣₂) is the verdict
after any further observations, so returning it early (line 11) is sound.
`OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.observeSet_correct_nonvacuous`: the hypotheses hold for an atom and one
observation holding time `0`, with the verdict its value. `OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.mk?_nodes_spec` is
line 2, the syntax tree (a DAG where a subformula repeats), and
`OnlineReachSetAnalyzer.observeLeaves_spec` lines 5-8.
