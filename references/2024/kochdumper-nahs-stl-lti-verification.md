# Fully Automated Verification of Linear Time-Invariant Systems against Signal Temporal Logic Specifications via Reachability Analysis

N. Kochdumper and S. Bak. "Fully Automated Verification of Linear Time-Invariant Systems against Signal Temporal Logic Specifications via Reachability Analysis". Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems, vol. 53, 2024, 101491.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.04089v2 (arXiv v2, 8 April 2024, 35 pages with the appendices; the preprint submitted to Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems, "April 9, 2024"; read in full, text extracted with `pypdf`, Defs. 5-6, Eq. (12) and the statement of Thm. 1 also rendered with PyMuPDF to read their symbols; ar5iv was not used). The published article, doi:10.1016/j.nahs.2024.101491, was not reachable from the environment: numbering is that of arXiv v2.  
**Inventory:** confirmed (Definitions 1-6, Propositions 1-5, Theorem 1, Algorithms 1-3, the numbered displays that make a claim, Eq. (26) among them, and Appendices A-B; the sections without a numbered result are rows of their own, named by section)

CORA's `linearSys/verify` with `'stl:kochdumper'` verifies a linear system against an STL formula by reachability analysis that keeps the dependencies of the reachable states on the initial state and the inputs, so that each polytope of the reachset temporal logic check of [Roehm et al. 2016](../2016/roehm-atva-stl-model-checking.md) becomes a polytope of *factors* of the initial set and the piecewise constant inputs, instead of a yes or no on the whole reachable set. The loop of [Wetzlinger et al. 2023](../2023/wetzlinger-fully-automated-verification.md), with the time step halved until the formula is proved or a violating factor is found. Here: the polytope of the factors that may reach a polytope (`Prop. 2`), the model checking that intersects them (`Alg. 2`), its soundness, the verifier that halves the time step (`Alg. 3`) and its soundness relative to its two oracles, and the program (26) that finds the violating factor. Not here: the reachability algorithm, its dependency preservation and its convergence (`Alg. 1`, `Prop. 1`, `Props. 3-5`), the conversion of the formula to reachset temporal logic, and the termination `Thm. 1`.

The formalization found three defects in the printed algorithms: the intersection of line 12 of `Alg. 2` is printed as a zip, the program (26) of `Alg. 3` is infeasible as printed, and the falsifying trajectory omits `B`; each has a Lean statement or is checked against CORA below.

## Findings

**`Alg. 2`, line 12 prints a zip.** The listing writes `V ← (V(1)∩W(1), ..., V(|V|)∩W(|W|))`. The disjunction over `(j,k)` of the formula is the intersection `⋃V ∩ ⋃W` of the unsafe factors so far and those of this pair, which is every `V(a)∩W(b)`, `|V|·|W|` polytopes, and the listing's `|V|` of them are the first pairs only: read that way two passes over `V = [ℝ]` with `W₁ = [{0},{1}]`, `W₂ = [{1},{0}]` give `[∅]`, where the factor `0` is in a polytope of each disjunct and violates the formula, and all pairs keep it ([`FactorMeetZipCounterexample.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Theorems/FactorMeetZipCounterexample.lean)). With all pairs the model checking is sound (`STL.factorCheck_sound`). CORA's `aux_orConstraints` forms all pairs `cons{i}{k} × list{j}`, so it runs the repaired listing. The class is `underspecified`: the intent is plain, the index is missing.

**`Eq. 26` is infeasible as printed.** For each polytope the program selects the one row `k` that `α̃` violates by the binaries `λ_jk` with `Σ_k λ_jk = 1`, and writes `α̃ = Σ_k α̃_jk` with `|α̃_jk| ≤ λ_jk`. It requires the strict `C_j(·,k) α̃_jk > λ_jk d_j(k)` of every `k`. A `k` with `λ_jk = 0` has `α̃_jk = 0` and the requirement `0 > 0`; a polytope of two or more halfspaces has such a `k`, so the program has no solution, whatever `α̃` and the polytope are, and `Alg. 3` never returns unsafe on it ([`MilpPrintedInfeasible.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Theorems/MilpPrintedInfeasible.lean): `STL.not_milpPrinted_of_two_le`; [`MilpPrintedCounterexample.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Theorems/MilpPrintedCounterexample.lean): the polytope `[-1/2,1/2]` and the factor `1` outside it). The repair requires the inequality where `λ_jk = 1`, which is solved exactly by the `α̃ ∈ [-1,1]^p` outside the polytope (`STL.milpCorrected_iff`). A strict inequality is not one a mixed-integer solver takes either; CORA's `aux_falsifyingFactors` encodes Eq. (9) of [5] there, a non-strict `C α̃ ≥ d` for the selected row. The notation `C_j(·,k)` is a column in the paper's own convention (Sec. 2.1) where the row `C_j(k,·)` is meant.

**The falsifying trajectory after (26) omits `B`.** `x(t_{i+1}) = e^{AΔt}x(t_i) + T(c_u + G_uα_{u,i})` with `T = A⁻¹(e^{AΔt} - I)`: with constant `u` on a step the solution of `ẋ = Ax + Bu` is `e^{AΔt}x(t_i) + TB u`, as Sec. 4's `r_u = Bc_u` and `U₀ = B(U ⊕ (-c_u))` have it, and the printed formula is that of `ẋ = Ax + u`. Its input factors are also shifted: `α̃` stores `α_{u,1}, ..., α_{u,t_end/Δt}` for the steps `[(j-1)Δt, jΔt]`, and the formula uses `α_{u,i}` for `i = 0, ..., t_end/Δt` to reach `x(t_{i+1})`: it should be `α_{u,i+1}` for `i = 0, ..., t_end/Δt - 1`. CORA's `aux_falsifyingTrajectory` returns `x₀ = c_x + G_xα_x` and the inputs `c_u + G_uα` and leaves the integration to the caller, so it is not affected. Not formalized: the trajectory formula is not a numbered result.

**`Prop. 1`'s `N_i` is one index too long.** `R(τ_i) = conv(H(t_i), H(t_{i+1})) ⊕ P(t_{i+1}) ⊕ C_i` has, by (8), `2γ_x + 1` generators of the convex hull (the `H`'s have `γ_x` generators each), then the `(i+1)γ_u` input generators of `P_c(t_{i+1})`, so the input generators of `N_i` are `2γ_x + 2, ..., 2γ_x + 1 + γ_u(i+1)`; the printed last index is `2γ_x + 2 + γ_u(i+1)`, the first generator of `𝒟`'s box. CORA's `ind.int{i} = [1:p_x, 2*p_x+2 : 2*p_x+i*p_u+1]`, its `i` being the paper's `i+1`, has the count right. `Prop. 1` is `—`, so this is read off the generator layout, not formalized.

**`Def. 6` and `Sec. 3` are typed loosely.** `∀t ∈ ⌊a/Δt⌋ + [0,Δt]` adds an index to times (`⌊a/Δt⌋Δt`), and `Sec. 3` asks `ξ(t,x₀,u(·)) ⊨ φ` for the state `ξ(t,...)` of `Def. 3` where `⊨` takes the trace `ξ(·,x₀,u(·))`, which makes the `∀t` superfluous. The Appendix A example writes `○₁` for the next operator at time `Δt`, `○_{0.5}` of `Def. 6`: its time unit is `Δt`. None changes a result.

**The convergence proofs of Appendix B take limits that do not compose.** `Prop. 3` and `Prop. 4` let `Δt → 0` in `𝒟` and `C_i` at a fixed index `i`, `Prop. 4` through `(e^{AΔt})^i → I`; the reach sequence's error is the sum of `i = t/Δt` terms, each of order `Δt²`, and `C_i = e^{At_i}(F X₀ ⊕ G r_u)` at the time `t_i`, where `(e^{A·0})^i = I` does not hold for `i = t/Δt → ∞`. The conclusions are true (`F`, `G` and `𝒟` vanish at the rates of [5, Lemmas 1-4], the sum is `O(Δt)`, and `‖e^{At_i}‖ ≤ e^{‖A‖t_end}`), but the printed arguments do not establish them, a `proof-gap` once the three propositions are formalized; they are `—` here.

**`Thm. 1` leaves "decidable" undefined.** The text before it says undecidable instances are those whose reachable set lies exactly on the decision boundary. For `ẋ = -x`, `X₀ = [-1,0]`, `φ = □[0,1] x ≤ 0`, the system satisfies `φ` with the initial state `0` on the boundary at all times; every enclosure of `Alg. 1` with `Δt > 0` has a positive error and meets `x > 0`, and `Alg. 3` does not stop. That is the boundary case the text excludes, and the statement does not say it: `Thm. 1` holds for the instances whose reachable set is at a positive distance from the unsafe set or a set of factors violating `φ` has non-empty interior, which the proof assumes when it lets the errors converge to 0. Not formalized.

**CORA's `aux_evaluatePredicate` may return a status of the last unsafe set only.** For a predicate that is a union of unsafe sets the function loops over them, storing the status of each in `list(i)`, and then returns `max(list(i))`, the status of the last set, not `max(list)`. If the reachable set meets an earlier unsafe set (status 2) and misses the last one (status 1), the predicate is marked satisfied on the whole reachable set (status 1). Modelled as printed in [`STL.evaluatePredicateUnionCora`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Operations/EvaluatePredicateUnionCora.lean), and [`STL.evaluatePredicateUnionCora_lastOnly_counterexample`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Theorems/EvaluatePredicateUnionLastOnlyCounterexample.lean) shows it for `R = [-1, 1]` against `x ≤ 0` and `x ≤ -5`: the first set alone gives status 2, the union status 1. The downstream effect (`sampledTime` turns status 1 into `true`) is read from the source, not formalized; the case needs a predicate that is a union of at least two unsafe sets (a safe set that is a union of polytopes, or a disjunction). `Alg. 3`'s line 4 is also not what CORA runs: it fixes the truncation order at 10 and halves `Δt` until the Taylor series of `T` and the remainder converge (`aux_initTimeStepSize`), where the paper tunes `κ` per `Δt` by the Frobenius norms of `T^(κ)` and `T^(κ+1)`.
