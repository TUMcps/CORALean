# Fully Automated Verification of Linear Time-Invariant Systems against Signal Temporal Logic Specifications via Reachability Analysis

N. Kochdumper and S. Bak. "Fully Automated Verification of Linear Time-Invariant Systems against Signal Temporal Logic Specifications via Reachability Analysis". Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems, vol. 53, 2024, 101491.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.04089v2 (arXiv v2, 8 April 2024, 35 pages with the appendices; the preprint submitted to Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems, "April 9, 2024"; read in full, text extracted with `pypdf`, Defs. 5-6, Eq. (12) and the statement of Thm. 1 also rendered with PyMuPDF to read their symbols; ar5iv was not used). The published article, doi:10.1016/j.nahs.2024.101491, was not reachable from the environment: numbering is that of arXiv v2.  
**Inventory:** confirmed (Definitions 1-6, Propositions 1-5, Theorem 1, Algorithms 1-3, the numbered displays that make a claim, Eq. (26) among them, and Appendices A-B; the sections without a numbered result are rows of their own, named by section)

CORA's `linearSys/verify` with `'stl:kochdumper'` verifies a linear system against an STL formula by reachability analysis that keeps the dependencies of the reachable states on the initial state and the inputs, so that each polytope of the reachset temporal logic check of [Roehm et al. 2016](../2016/roehm-atva-stl-model-checking.md) becomes a polytope of *factors* of the initial set and the piecewise constant inputs, instead of a yes or no on the whole reachable set. The loop of [Wetzlinger et al. 2023](../2023/wetzlinger-fully-automated-verification.md), with the time step halved until the formula is proved or a violating factor is found. Here: the polytope of the factors that may reach a polytope (`Prop. 2`), the model checking that intersects them (`Alg. 2`), its soundness, the verifier that halves the time step (`Alg. 3`) and its soundness relative to its two oracles, and the program (26) that finds the violating factor. Also here: the reachability algorithm (`Alg. 1`, `Eq. 14-20`) as a definition, the solution at its time points as the dependency of `Prop. 1` and its failure between them and in `K_i2` of `Prop. 2`, the definitions `Def. 4` and `Sec. 3`. Also here: the convergence of the enclosures (`Props. 3-5`). Not here: the soundness of the enclosures (16)-(19), the conversion of the formula to reachset temporal logic, and the termination `Thm. 1`.

The formalization found three defects in the printed algorithms: the intersection of line 12 of `Alg. 2` is printed as a zip, the program (26) of `Alg. 3` is infeasible as printed, and the falsifying trajectory omits `B`; each has a Lean statement or is checked against CORA below. It also found that `Prop. 1` is false between the time points and that `Prop. 2` is false for the time intervals with it, `K_i2`.

## Findings

**`Alg. 2`, line 12 prints a zip.** The listing writes `V ← (V(1)∩W(1), ..., V(|V|)∩W(|W|))`. The disjunction over `(j,k)` of the formula is the intersection `⋃V ∩ ⋃W` of the unsafe factors so far and those of this pair, which is every `V(a)∩W(b)`, `|V|·|W|` polytopes, and the listing's `|V|` of them are the first pairs only: read that way two passes over `V = [ℝ]` with `W₁ = [{0},{1}]`, `W₂ = [{1},{0}]` give `[∅]`, where the factor `0` is in a polytope of each disjunct and violates the formula, and all pairs keep it ([`FactorMeetZip.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Counterexamples/FactorMeetZip.lean)). With all pairs the model checking is sound (`STL.factorCheck_sound`). CORA's `aux_orConstraints` forms all pairs `cons{i}{k} × list{j}`, so it runs the repaired listing. The class is `underspecified`: the intent is plain, the index is missing.

**`Eq. 26` is infeasible as printed.** For each polytope the program selects the one row `k` that `α̃` violates by the binaries `λ_jk` with `Σ_k λ_jk = 1`, and writes `α̃ = Σ_k α̃_jk` with `|α̃_jk| ≤ λ_jk`. It requires the strict `C_j(·,k) α̃_jk > λ_jk d_j(k)` of every `k`. A `k` with `λ_jk = 0` has `α̃_jk = 0` and the requirement `0 > 0`; a polytope of two or more halfspaces has such a `k`, so the program has no solution, whatever `α̃` and the polytope are, and `Alg. 3` never returns unsafe on it ([`MilpPrintedInfeasible.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Theorems/MilpPrintedInfeasible.lean): `STL.not_milpPrinted_of_two_le`; [`MilpPrinted.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Counterexamples/MilpPrinted.lean): the polytope `[-1/2,1/2]` and the factor `1` outside it). The repair requires the inequality where `λ_jk = 1`, which is solved exactly by the `α̃ ∈ [-1,1]^p` outside the polytope (`STL.milpCorrected_iff`). A strict inequality is not one a mixed-integer solver takes either; CORA's `aux_falsifyingFactors` encodes Eq. (9) of [5] there, a non-strict `C α̃ ≥ d` for the selected row. The notation `C_j(·,k)` is a column in the paper's own convention (Sec. 2.1) where the row `C_j(k,·)` is meant.

**The falsifying trajectory after (26) omits `B`.** `x(t_{i+1}) = e^{AΔt}x(t_i) + T(c_u + G_uα_{u,i})` with `T = A⁻¹(e^{AΔt} - I)`: with constant `u` on a step the solution of `ẋ = Ax + Bu` is `e^{AΔt}x(t_i) + TB u`, as Sec. 4's `r_u = Bc_u` and `U₀ = B(U ⊕ (-c_u))` have it, and the printed formula is that of `ẋ = Ax + u`. Its input factors are also shifted: `α̃` stores `α_{u,1}, ..., α_{u,t_end/Δt}` for the steps `[(j-1)Δt, jΔt]`, and the formula uses `α_{u,i}` for `i = 0, ..., t_end/Δt` to reach `x(t_{i+1})`: it should be `α_{u,i+1}` for `i = 0, ..., t_end/Δt - 1`. CORA's `aux_falsifyingTrajectory` returns `x₀ = c_x + G_xα_x` and the inputs `c_u + G_uα` and leaves the integration to the caller, so it is not affected. Not formalized: the trajectory formula is not a numbered result.

**`Prop. 1`'s `N_i` is one index too long.** `R(τ_i) = conv(H(t_i), H(t_{i+1})) ⊕ P(t_{i+1}) ⊕ C_i` has, by (8), `2γ_x + 1` generators of the convex hull (the `H`'s have `γ_x` generators each), then the `(i+1)γ_u` input generators of `P_c(t_{i+1})`, so the input generators of `N_i` are `2γ_x + 2, ..., 2γ_x + 1 + γ_u(i+1)`; the printed last index is `2γ_x + 2 + γ_u(i+1)`, the first generator of `𝒟`'s box. CORA's `ind.int{i} = [1:p_x, 2*p_x+2 : 2*p_x+i*p_u+1]`, its `i` being the paper's `i+1`, has the count right. `Prop. 1` is `—`, so this is read off the generator layout, not formalized.

**`Prop. 1` is false at a time between two time points.** At `t = t_i` the solution of the piecewise constant input is `μ + c_{i1}` (`STL.depReachPoint_dependency`), because `P_c(t_{i+1}) = e^{AΔt}P_c(t_i) ⊕ T U₀` is the exact solution of `ẋ = Ax + Bu` under that input. For `t ∈ τ_i` the same `μ` is printed: `μ = [G_{i2}(·,N_i) 0] α̃` keeps the generators of `P_c(t_{i+1})`, which are the input's effect at `t_{i+1}`, while the state at `t` has only reached `(t - t_i)/Δt` of the last step's, and `E(t)` holds the hull's `½(G₁-G₂)` and `½(c₁-c₂)`, `D_{i+1}` and `C_i`, none of which are of the first order in `Δt` for a step of the input. For `ẋ = u` (`A = 0`, where `E`, `F`, `G` and `D` vanish), `X₀ = {0}`, `U = [-1,1]`, `Δt = 1`, `i = 0`: `R(τ₀) = [-1,1]` has the generators `(0, 1, 0, 0)` (the hull's `½(c₁-c₂)`, `T B G_u`, `D₁`, `C₀`), `N₀ = (2)` and `E` is `{0}`, the factor `α_{u,1} = 1` gives `μ = 1` and `ξ(1/2) = 1/2` ([`DepReachIntervalDependency.lean`](../../src/CORALean/Specification/STL/Counterexamples/DepReachIntervalDependency.lean): `STL.depReachInterval_dependency_counterexample`). The half for the time points holds: `hξ` of `STL.not_mem_of_not_mem_unsafeFactors` is `Prop. 1` at `t_i`, which `STL.depReachPoint_dependency` proves, and `STL.not_mem_of_not_mem_unsafeFactors_point` is `Prop. 2` for `K_i1`. **`Def. 4` is satisfied by every enclosure.** "Can be used to construct" a function `μ` and an error set `E` from `R(t)` has no formal content; read as an existence it holds for every `R` with `ξ ∈ R` (`μ = ξ`, `E = {0}`, `STL.dependencyPreserved_of_encloses`), the one thing it excludes being `μ = 0` with `E = R` for a set that is not a point. The property of `Alg. 1` that [1] uses is that `μ` is `[G(·,H) 0] α̃` for the generators of its own `R`, which is what `Prop. 1` states.

**`Def. 6` and `Sec. 3` are typed loosely.** `∀t ∈ ⌊a/Δt⌋ + [0,Δt]` adds an index to times (`⌊a/Δt⌋Δt`), and `Sec. 3` asks `ξ(t,x₀,u(·)) ⊨ φ` for the state `ξ(t,...)` of `Def. 3` where `⊨` takes the trace `ξ(·,x₀,u(·))`, which makes the `∀t` superfluous. The Appendix A example writes `○₁` for the next operator at time `Δt`, `○_{0.5}` of `Def. 6`: its time unit is `Δt`. None changes a result.

**The convergence proofs of Appendix B take limits that do not compose.** `Prop. 3` is a limit of an explicit formula and is correct (`STL.tendsto_depBloating_zero`). `Prop. 4` lets `Δt → 0` in `C_i` at a fixed index `i`, through `(e^{AΔt})^i → I`; and `Prop. 5` sets `Δt = 0` in `H(t_{i+1}) = e^{AΔt}H(t_i) ⊕ T r_u` with `H(t_i)` held fixed. The reach sequence has `i = t/Δt` steps, so `i → ∞` with `Δt → 0`, where `(e^{A·0})^i = I` does not apply, `C_i = e^{At_i}(F X₀ ⊕ G r_u)` is taken at the time `t_i`, and `H(t_i)` is a different set for every `Δt`; and equal limits of the hull and its enclosure do not make the distance between them vanish without a continuity statement. The conclusions are true, uniformly in the steps up to a final time `T`: `F`, `G` and `𝒟` vanish at the rates of [5, Lemmas 1-4], `‖e^{AΔt}‖^i ≤ e^{T‖A‖}`, and the distance of the hull enclosure is `√γ_x ‖(e^{AΔt} - I)G_i‖₂`. The printed arguments do not establish them, a `proof-gap` for `Props. 4-5`, whose Lean theorems are the uniform ones (`STL.tendsto_depReachAt_C_zero`, `STL.tendsto_depReach_linComb_hausdorff_zero`). The error of the whole sequence, the sum of the `i` terms `𝒟_i`, is not covered by `Prop. 3`, which is about one `𝒟`; it is not stated as a result of [1] and not formalized.

**`Thm. 1` leaves "decidable" undefined.** The text before it says undecidable instances are those whose reachable set lies exactly on the decision boundary. For `ẋ = -x`, `X₀ = [-1,0]`, `φ = □[0,1] x ≤ 0`, the system satisfies `φ` with the initial state `0` on the boundary at all times; every enclosure of `Alg. 1` with `Δt > 0` has a positive error and meets `x > 0`, and `Alg. 3` does not stop. That is the boundary case the text excludes, and the statement does not say it: `Thm. 1` holds for the instances whose reachable set is at a positive distance from the unsafe set or a set of factors violating `φ` has non-empty interior, which the proof assumes when it lets the errors converge to 0. Not formalized.

**CORA's `aux_evaluatePredicate` may return a status of the last unsafe set only.** For a predicate that is a union of unsafe sets the function loops over them, storing the status of each in `list(i)`, and then returns `max(list(i))`, the status of the last set, not `max(list)`. If the reachable set meets an earlier unsafe set (status 2) and misses the last one (status 1), the predicate is marked satisfied on the whole reachable set (status 1).
