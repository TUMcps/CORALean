# Zonotope/Hyperplane Intersection for Hybrid Systems Reachability Analysis

A. Girard and C. Le Guernic. "Zonotope/Hyperplane Intersection for Hybrid Systems Reachability Analysis". In Hybrid Systems: Computation and Control (HSCC), LNCS 4981, Springer, 2008, pp. 215-228.

**Source:** https://www.cecs.uci.edu/~papers/cpsweek08/papers/hscc08/session1/s1-2.pdf (the authors' manuscript in the LNCS format from the HSCC 2008 proceedings, 14 pages, read in full; text extracted with `pypdf`; the Springer DOI is 10.1007/978-3-540-78929-1_16)  
**Inventory:** confirmed (the numbered items are Proposition 1, Algorithms 1-4, Remarks 1-2 and the display of equation (1); the unnumbered statements that make a claim are rows named by section)

The paper behind CORA's `guardIntersect_zonoGirard` (`location/guardIntersect`'s `'zonoGirard'` method): the intersection of a reachable-set zonotope with a guard hyperplane is over-approximated in finitely many directions `D` by projecting onto the plane spanned by the hyperplane's normal `n` and a direction `ℓ` (`Prop. 1`) and bounding the line `x = γ` against the plane image (`Alg. 2`), with the line routine `BOUND_INTERSECT_2D` of `Alg. 3` and `Alg. 4`. CORA takes `D` to be the directions of a basis (`Sec. 3.4`) and returns a zonotope bundle of the boxes. Formalized here: the intersection test, the projection identity, the dimension-reduction step with `BOUND_INTERSECT_2D` as an argument that owes the output specification of `Alg. 3` and `Alg. 4` (`IsBoundIntersect2D`), that a polyhedron with orthonormal normals is a zonotope, and both line routines, each as printed with a correctness theorem. They rest on the edge structure of a zonogon: for generators in the half-plane `y > 0 ∨ (y = 0 ∧ x ≥ 0)` sorted by angle the zonogon is the convex hull of the corners of the planar vertex walk ([`ConstructEqConvexHullWalk.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Zonotope/Real/Theorems/ConstructEqConvexHullWalk.lean)), whose edges `[P; P + 2g]` are the two chains `Alg. 3` scans and, for nonzero generators, its boundary ([`FrontierConstructEqWalkEdges.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Zonotope/Real/Theorems/FrontierConstructEqWalkEdges.lean)), and its intersection with a line `x = γ` is the segment between the two edges that cross it ([`SectionEqIccWalkEdges.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Zonotope/Real/Theorems/SectionEqIccWalkEdges.lean)). CORA's method is formalized with `BOUND_INTERSECT_2D` replaced by the Lagrangian dual of the same linear program, a bound on the cut proved outer only ([`GuardIntersectZonoGirard.lean`](../../src/CORALean/HybridDynamics/Definitions/GuardIntersectZonoGirard.lean), [`GuardIntersectZonoGirardOuter.lean`](../../src/CORALean/HybridDynamics/Theorems/GuardIntersectZonoGirardOuter.lean)); the two algorithms are not wired into it, and their printed pseudocode needed four completions, see Findings.

## Findings

**`Alg. 3` needs a generator, and line 12 names a point where a vertical edge has a segment.** For
`r = 0` the loops do not run and the output is `[∞; -∞]`, though `Z = {c}` meets `L_γ` when
`c_x = γ`: `{γ} × [m; M] = Z ∩ L_γ` needs `r ≥ 1` (with all generators zero the edges are the
point `c` and the scan is right). Line 12, `(x, y) ← [P; P + 2g] ∩ L_γ`, takes the intersection to
be a point, which a vertical edge on `L_γ` is not; read as every point of it (the least `y` for
`m`, the greatest for `M`) the scan is right, and a single `y` is wrong only when all the
generators are vertical, e.g. `r = 1`, since otherwise the ends of a vertical edge are ends of its
neighbours. The sort of line 9 is by `Complex.arg`, which gives a zero generator (no direction)
the angle `0` and leaves parallel generators in any order; their edges are collinear and the proof
does not depend on it.

**`Alg. 4` needs `Z ∩ L_γ ≠ ∅`.** The loop starts from "`[P; P + ∑ 2g]` intersects `L_γ`" (lines 9-14) and
keeps it, and line 27 reads a point off `[P; P + s] ∩ L_γ`; for an empty intersection the output
`{γ} × [m; M] = Z ∩ L_γ` has no real `m`, where `Alg. 3` returns `[∞; -∞]`. `Alg. 2` calls the
routine only on a nonempty cut; the correctness theorem takes it as a hypothesis. `G =
{g₁, …, g_r} ∩ (ℝ₊ × ℝ)` is read with a strict sign, so a vertical generator is in neither side.

**`Alg. 4`'s left branch takes "smaller" in the reverse order.** Lines 9-13 look right of the
lowest vertex if `x_P < γ` and left otherwise, and lines 14-26 are the same for both. From the
lowest vertex the walk meets the generators pointing right in increasing angle, so the smaller
half `G<` (line 16) is its first part and `[P; P + s₁]` is a chord of the lower-right boundary
(`x` increases along it). It meets those pointing left in decreasing angle, and the smaller half
is its far end: `P + s₁` is then an interior point, the test of line 18 follows a chord that is
not on the boundary, and the search ends on the other boundary. On `c = 0`,
`g₁ = (-2, 1)`, `g₂ = (-1, 2)` the zonogon has the vertices `(3, -3)`, `(-1, -1)`, `(-3, 3)`,
`(1, 1)`; for `γ = -1` the lowest point is `(-1, -1)`, but the printed order passes the chord
`[(3, -3); (1, 1)]`, which misses the line, and ends on the edge `[(1, 1); (-3, 3)]`, returning
the highest point `(-1, 2)`. With "smaller" in the order of the walk, increasing angle to the
right and decreasing to the left, the search finds the lowest point on both sides
([`BisectLowIsLeast.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Zonotope/Real/Theorems/BisectLowIsLeast.lean)).

**`SPLIT_PIVOT` is not defined on parallel generators.** The pivot is the sum of the remaining
generators, so its angle is strictly between the extreme angles of `G` unless all of `G` is
parallel to it (the cross products of the generators with the pivot sum to zero, so they are not
all of one sign); then no generator is smaller or bigger, `G<` is empty, `s₁ = 0`, the pass changes
nothing and the `while` never ends. `splitPivot` takes the generator of least index off in that
case, which is order-free since all cross products vanish
([`SplitPivotSpec.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Zonotope/Real/Theorems/SplitPivotSpec.lean)).
Line 29, "the same thing for `M`, starting from the upper vertex", is the search on the
zonogon mirrored in the x-axis, negated.
