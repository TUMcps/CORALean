In `ℝ¹` with `A = C = r = 1` CORA's `Sigma` is `(1 − e⁻²)/2 ≈ 0.43`, the true variance `(e² − 1)/2 ≈ 3.19` (read from the source, not run; the formula is [the ECC finding](../../2009/althoff-ecc-enclosing-hulls.md) itself). The theorems are at a diagonal `A`. Real layer (`noncomputable`), not float. Proved: for `A = V diag(λ) V⁻¹` (`V` invertible, real eigenvalues, given as `eig`'s output) `pRinput`'s covariance is the noise covariance `Σ_s(r)` exactly and the `Rpar_prob` handed to `post` has covariance `0 = Σ_s(0)`, so after `k` `post` steps it is `Σ_s(kr)` and the next time-interval set's `Σ_s(r + kr)`; and the means are enclosed: for `x₀ ∈ Rinit`'s mean set, `t ∈ [0, r]` and any measurable input in `B U`, `e^{At} x₀ + ∫₀ᵗ e^{Aσ}dσ B uTrans + ∫₀ᵗ e^{A(t−s)} u(s) ds` is in `Rfirst.ti`'s set (and at `t = r` in `Rfirst.tp`'s), for `A` invertible, Taylor order `≥ 2` with `‖Ar‖_∞/(η + 2) < 1`, `0 ∈ U`, and `B uTrans = 0` whenever `originContained`. The system is the matrices `A`, `B`, `C` (`C` square, as `inputArgsCheck` asks). A run, `IsTrajectory`, is given by its law: at every time `t ≥ 0` the state is `N(e^{At} x₀ + ∫₀ᵗ e^{A(t−s)} B u(s) ds, Σ_s(t))`, `Σ_s(t) = noiseCovariance A C t` (`LinearSys`), from a point `x₀` as `simulate` starts, the law of the solution by [the ECC paper's Eq. 2](../../2009/althoff-ecc-enclosing-hulls.md). That is a definition, not a theorem: Mathlib has Brownian motion (`IsBrownianReal`) but neither its existence nor a stochastic integral, so the solution driven by a Brownian motion is not defined. Read off it: the mean and the covariance `Σ_s(t)` of a run, and that a run is in the `γ`-confidence set of `(Z, Σ_s(t))` with probability at least `P[−γ ≤ N(0, 1) ≤ γ]ⁿ` whenever its mean is in `Z`. The theorem is that covariance over two steps, `Σ(s + t) = e^{As} Σ(t) e^{Aᵀs} + Σ(s)`, exact, entry by entry |
