# Constrained zonotopes: A new tool for set-based estimation and fault detection

J. K. Scott, D. M. Raimondo, G. R. Marseglia and R. D. Braatz. "Constrained zonotopes: A new tool for set-based estimation and fault detection". Automatica, 69:126-136, 2016.

**Source:** https://web.mit.edu/braatzgroup/Scott_Automatica_2016.pdf (the authors' group copy of the published article, 11 pages, read in full; text extracted with `pypdf`)  
**Inventory:** confirmed (the numbered items are Definitions 1-3, Propositions 1-5, Theorem 1, Remarks 1-2 and the appendix's Algorithm 1; each of Definition, Proposition and Remark has its own counter)

Where `ConZonotope` comes from: a zonotope whose coefficients also satisfy `Aξ = b`, closed
under linear maps, Minkowski sums and intersection, and equal to the class of polytopes. The
formalization keeps the paper's exact closure identities, the interval-refinement
algorithm, emptiness and membership by one linear program, the lifted zonotope and the
enclosure statements of its complexity reduction (rescaling, constraint elimination,
lift-then-reduce), and `Thm. 1` in both directions, the appendix's interval algorithm with
both its outputs, its elimination estimate and its new zonotope reduction; the estimation and
fault-detection applications are not formalized.
[Raghuraman and Koeln](../2022/raghuraman-koeln-constrained-zonotopes.md) restate the
appendix algorithm as their Alg. 1 and the elimination as their Thm. 2.

## Findings

**`Alg. 1` — (A.1) does not hold of Algorithm 1's `R`.** The appendix says Algorithm 1 "also
returns `R ≡ [ρ^L, ρ^U]` satisfying `R_j ⊃ {ξ_j : Aξ = b, |ξ_i| ≤ 1, ∀ i ≠ j}`" (A.1). Step (2)
computes `R_j` from the running `E_k`, and an earlier step may already have tightened `E_k` using
`|ξ_j| ≤ 1`. On the single row `−2ξ₁ + ξ₂ = 0`, step `(1, 1)` gives `E₁ = [−½, ½]` from `|ξ₂| ≤ 1`,
and step `(1, 2)` gives `R₂ = 2E₁ = [−1, 1]`. But `ξ = (1, 2)` satisfies the row with `|ξ₁| ≤ 1`,
and `ξ₂ = 2 ∉ R₂`
([`TightenERNotSuperset.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/ConZonotope/Real/Theorems/TightenERNotSuperset.lean)).
Preconditioning to reduced row echelon form does not help: the row is already `ξ₁ − ½ξ₂ = 0` up
to scaling. What `R` does contain, after any number of iterations, is the projection of
`B∞(A, b)` ([`TightenEROuter.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/ConZonotope/Real/Theorems/TightenEROuter.lean)),
and that is all the emptiness test needs.

**`Sec. 4.2 (choice of j)` — `r_j = 0` does not make the elimination exact.** To select `j` the
appendix first checks `r_j = 0`, `r_j = max(0, max(|ρ^L_j|, |ρ^U_j|) − 1)`, "if this holds, then
(A.1) and (A.4) imply that `Z = Z̃` when `j` is selected". The inference is valid, and it is proved
with (A.1) as its hypothesis
([`PartialSolveEqOfRZero.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/ConZonotope/Real/Theorems/PartialSolveEqOfRZero.lean)),
but the `R` it is applied to is Algorithm 1's, which need not satisfy (A.1). On the system above
with `G = I`, `c = 0`, Algorithm 1 gives `R₂ = [−1, 1]`, so `r₂ = 0`. `Z` is the segment from
`(−½, −1)` to `(½, 1)`, while `Z̃ = {ξ : ξ₂ = 2ξ₁, |ξ₁| ≤ 1}` by (A.4) is the segment from
`(−1, −2)` to `(1, 2)`
([`PartialSolveRZeroCounterexample.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/ConZonotope/Real/Theorems/PartialSolveRZeroCounterexample.lean)).
The test is sound with an `R_j` that does enclose (A.1)'s set.
