Inner approximation of the range of `f : ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ`, the first method for a vector-valued `f`.
A box `y` that misses the image of the boundary of `x` and of the singular points of `f`
and meets `f(x)` is inside it (`Thm. 3.1`, from the inverse function theorem and the
connectedness of `y`); a Hansen-Sengupta-type inclusion `x̃ + Γ(J, x - x̃, y - f(x̃)) ⊆ int x`
proves that (`Cor. 3.1`), also after preconditioning (3), (4). Formalized in `ℝⁿ` for boxes,
without Brouwer's theorem: the lemmas of the appendix up to `Lemma A.2`, `Thm. 3.1`,
`Cor. 3.1` with its H-matrix claim, (3) and (4), and both worked examples. The bisection
`Alg. 1` and the domain inflation `Alg. 2` are the ones CORA runs for the point map of a
polynomial zonotope (`PolyZonotope.innerApproxImage`, `PolyZonotope.innerSpecial`), whose
accepted boxes are inside the image by Brouwer's theorem, since `Alg. 2` tests the closed
inclusion. The asymptotic convergence `Thm. 5.1` and the technical lemmas it rests on, Lipschitz
estimates for interval functions and a Kaucher-arithmetic fixed point, are not formalized.

## Findings

**No printed result is false.** `Lemma A.1`, `Lemma A.2`, `Thm. 3.1`, `Cor. 3.1`, (3), (4) and the two worked examples hold as printed, in `Fin n → ℝ` with boxes `Icc`; the Lean statements are the paper's up to the following readings. The paper's `f` is continuously differentiable in `int x` and continuous on `x`; the Lean takes a strict derivative `f'` at the interior points, which a `C¹` function has. `J` contains `f'(x)` "for `x ∈ x`", but `f'` need not exist on `∂x`; it is read on `int x`, which is where the proof uses it: (2) at `v = x̃` puts `x̃` in `int x`, so the segment from `x̃` to a boundary point is interior up to its end and the mean-value theorem applies. The interval evaluation of (2) is exact (each of `Jᵢᵢ`, `Jᵢⱼ`, `vⱼ`, `yᵢ` occurs once), so `PassesGammaTest` over the real points loses nothing.

**`Cor. 3.1` — what the proof does with (2).** The printed proof checks (i) on all of `∂x`, which works because `y` has the full dimension `n` of `x` (the faces where only some coordinates are at a bound are all of `∂x` and (2) bounds `vᵢ` for every `i`); it is the extension to `m < n` components of Mullier et al. that breaks at that step (`innerTest_step_counterexample`, see the Mullier page). (ii) is `Σ = ∅`, by the H-matrix scaling `wid x`: row `i` of the real matrix with the smallest diagonal and the largest off-diagonal magnitudes, evaluated at the two corners of `x` that extremise the off-diagonal sum, gives two points of `(xloᵢ, xhiᵢ)` that differ by `Σ_{j≠i} \|Jᵢⱼ\| widⱼ / \|Jᵢᵢ\|`, which is therefore `< widᵢ`.

**`Alg. 2` — the closed inclusion.** Line 8 returns True on `x̃ + u ⊆ x̃`, and the text justifies this by `Cor. 3.1`, which needs `⊆ int x̃`. The domain inflation does not make the inclusion strict, so the justification falls short; the claim is nonetheless true, for the reason of the Mullier page: on the slice at `ũ₂` (here the whole space) the closed test is a continuous self-map of the box and Brouwer's theorem fixes a zero (`InnerRangeTestClosed.lean`, `PolyZonotope.innerSpecial_subset_image`). It needs the midpoint to stay in the inflated box, which `0 ∈ b` gives in exact arithmetic (`ỹ` contains `f(mid x̃)`); CORALean checks it.

**`Thm. 5.1` and the appendix lemmas.** Not formalized, and not blocked by a missing Mathlib result of the kind Brouwer's theorem was: the proof is a quantitative estimate. It needs interval functions with the Hausdorff metric on `IRⁿ`, Lipschitz constants of `Γ` in the matrix and in the box (`Lemma A.3`, `Lemma A.4`, `Prop. A.2`), the bounds on `f'(x)⁻¹` near a regular point (`Prop. A.1`), the Banach fixed-point theorem for the inflation map `h` on `Ix⁺`, and, for the existence of the right-hand side `b⁺` with `Γ(A, x, b) = x`, Kaucher arithmetic (`Lemma A.5`; `KaucherInner.lean` has Kaucher arithmetic for a different purpose). Also, the proof of `Thm. 5.1` takes a sequence of boxes `x⁽ᵏ⁾ ∋ x*` of width `≤ 1/k`, which presumes that the bisection of `Alg. 1` halves a widest coordinate; the algorithm only says "bisect".

**CORA.** `innerApprox` and `innerApproxImage` follow `Alg. 1` and `Alg. 2`: `ỹ = f(x̃) & (f(c) + df(x̃)(x̃ - c))`, `C = inv(df(c))`, `b = C ỹ - C f(c)`, `τ = 1.01`, `μ = 0.9`, the closed `contains(x̃, c + u)` at line 8. The deviations are those listed on the `Alg. 1` row: the stop test is `min(rad(x̃)) >= tol` where the paper has `‖rad x̃‖ ≥ ε` (for a widest-coordinate bisection the two differ by at most a factor 2), the boundary list `LBoundary` is not kept, and `tol` defaults to `max(rad(X))/100`. No bug found.
