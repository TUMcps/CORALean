**Source:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.08157 (arXiv v5 of 22 June 2018, the CDC version, 8 pages, read in full, text extracted with `pypdf`, the CDC DOI is 10.1109/CDC.2018.8619508)  
**Inventory:** confirmed (the numbered items are Lemmas 1-2, Proposition 1, Corollary 1, Theorems 2-3, Remark 1 and equations (1)-(28); the paper has no Definitions, and the unnumbered statements that make a claim are rows named by section)

The paper behind the `'outer:halder'` mode of CORA's `ellipsoid/plus` (`priv_plusEllipsoidOA_halder`): the outer ellipsoid of two ellipsoids with shape `Q(β) = (1 + 1/β) Q₁ + (1 + β) Q₂` of minimum volume over `β > 0`, whose optimality condition is a scalar equation in the eigenvalues of `Q₁⁻¹Q₂` with a unique positive root, found by a monotone fixed-point iteration. The tree's `Ellipsoid.plus` takes the two generator scalings `√(1+1/β)`, `√(1+β)` of this family as arguments (`shape_plus` reads off the shape `Q(β)`); the scalar analysis is stated for the eigenvalues `λᵢ` and tied to the matrices by `log_det_halder_eq`. CORA's loop runs the step from `β = 0.5` until two iterates agree within a tolerance, which is not a function of the data, so the formalization has the step and proves the limit. The proof of the uniqueness of the root rests on two false claims about the coefficients of its polynomial form (`Lemma 2`, `Cor. 1`), and the expanded polynomial `Eq. 24` is misprinted; none of the results used is affected, since the root is unique by a direct monotonicity argument. The proof of the convergence of the iteration is a contraction argument that does not apply.

## Findings

**`Lemma 2` and `Cor. 1` — the coefficients are neither increasing nor positive.** Expanding (22), with `P(β) = Πⱼ (β + 1/λⱼ) = Σ eᵣ β^{d-r}`, gives `Σᵢ (β² - 1/λᵢ) Πⱼ≠ᵢ (β + 1/λⱼ) = (β² + β) P'(β) - d P(β)`, whose coefficient of `β^{d+1-r}` is `µᵣ = (d - r) eᵣ - (r - 1) eᵣ₋₁` of (23), with `µ₀ = d`, `µ_d = -(d - 1) e_{d-1}` and `µ_{d+1} = -d e_d`. The proof of `Lemma 2` computes `µ₂ - µ₁ = (d - 2)(e₂ + e₁)` and in general `µᵣ₊₁ - µᵣ = (d - r - 1) eᵣ₊₁ + (d - 2r) eᵣ + (r - 1) eᵣ₋₁`; by (23) they are `(d - 2) e₂ - d e₁` and `(d - r - 1) eᵣ₊₁ - d eᵣ + (r - 1) eᵣ₋₁`, so the sign argument of the proof does not apply, and the statements fail. For `d = 3` and `λ = (1, 1, 10)`, `e₁ = 21/10` and `e₂ = 6/5`, so `µ₁ = 21/5` and `µ₂ = -9/10`: the sequence falls (`Lemma 2`) and `µ₂` is negative (`Cor. 1`); equally at `λ = (1, 1, 1)`, `µ₁ = 6 > µ₂ = 0`. The Lean statements quantify over `d`, `λ` and `r` as the paper does and are refuted at that instance ([`HalderMuIncreasing.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Ellipsoid/Real/Counterexamples/HalderMuIncreasing.lean), [`HalderMuPositive.lean`](../../src/CORALean/ContSet/Ellipsoid/Real/Counterexamples/HalderMuPositive.lean)). Neither is repairable as a sign statement: the coefficients `µ₂, …, µ_{d-1}` take either sign.

**`Eq. 24` — a term counted twice.** The expanded polynomial prints `(d - 1) e₁ β^d` and then `Σ_{r=1}^{d-1} µᵣ β^{d-r+1}`, whose `r = 1` term is `µ₁ β^d = (d - 1) e₁ β^d` again; the sum should start at `r = 2`. At `d = 2` and `λ = (1, 1)`, `β = 1` the printed expansion is `2 + 2 + 2 - 2 - 2 = 2`, while (22) is `0` there (`printed_poly_counterexample`; the correct `2β³ + e₁β² - e₁β - 2e₂` is `0`). The corrected expansion is proved for every `d`: the left side of (22) is `dβ^{d+1} + Σ_{r=1}^{d+1} µᵣ β^{d+1-r}` (`halder_poly_expand`). **`Thm. 2` — a correct statement, an unsupported proof.** The proof reads the signs of the coefficients of (24) off `Cor. 1` and concludes by Descartes' rule that there is one sign change. With `Cor. 1` false the coefficients need not have a single sign change on the printed argument; the root is nevertheless unique, since each term `(1 - β²λᵢ)/(1 + βλᵢ)` of (18) is strictly decreasing in `β ≥ 0` (the difference of two values is `(b - a) λ (1 + (a + b) + abλ)` over the denominators), the sum is `d > 0` at `0` and negative at `1 + Σ 1/λᵢ`, where every term is. That is the Lean proof. **`Thm. 3` — the contraction argument.** The proof states that `g` is concave and increasing on `ℝ₊`, hence "contractive in Hilbert metric on the cone `ℝ₊`", and applies Banach's fixed-point theorem. Hilbert's projective metric is identically zero on a one-dimensional cone, since any two positive reals are proportional, so contractivity in it says nothing about the iterates. The theorem is true: `g` is monotone on `[0, ∞)` (`A(x) B(y) ≤ A(y) B(x)` for `x ≤ y`, a double sum whose terms pair off as `-(y - x)(a - b)²` over the denominators), `g(β) > β` exactly where (18) is positive, and `g` is continuous, so a bounded monotone orbit converges to a fixed point, which is the root.
