(`'pca'`, "Sec. III.A in [3]" in CORA's own header). the transformation methods it surveys are restated from earlier work.

## Findings

**`Alg. 3` — the listing needs L invertible and leaves three things open.** Line 13 forms `L⁻¹ Z`, but line 2 draws `n` random generators of `G`, which are linearly dependent whenever two are parallel (and a cluster can then collapse), so `L` can be singular and the listing says nothing about it. Line 12 divides by the number of generators in cluster `k`, which is zero for an empty cluster. And line 2 is random, so the algorithm is not a function of `Z`. The formalization (`lineClBasis`) supplies them: run `t` starts at the `n` dropped generators from index `t`; an empty cluster has a zero scatter matrix and keeps an eigenvector of it (line 12, a positive rescaling of the columns, is not carried out since `P · IH(P⁻¹ Z)` is unchanged by it). A singular candidate is never kept (`lineClKeep`), and when every run is singular the basis is the identity. With that, `reduceLineCl_outer` holds at every `L`, as `reduceByBasis_outer` is stated for an arbitrary invertible matrix; the choice of clustering decides tightness only, and nothing here claims it is tighter than the box.

**`Alg. 4` — the solver decides, and its answer is checked.** Lines 3-5 hand eq. 11 subject to eq. 13 to MATLAB's `fmincon`, whose return is not guaranteed to satisfy the constraint (it can stop at an infeasible point), and eq. 13 inverts `diag(sᵢᵢ)` while eq. 10 inverts `C` without saying either must be invertible. A singular `C` with `C⁻¹` read as anything bounded satisfies eq. 10 vacuously and encloses nothing. The formalization (`reduceCoOptSVD`, `reduceCoOptDir`) takes the solver as an argument, keeps its candidate only when it is invertible and meets the constraint, and otherwise returns the PCA initialisation of line 1, which encloses `Z` on its own; line 2's decomposition of that initialisation is PCA's own factorisation `U₀ diag(S₀) Iᵀ`. `reduceCoOptSVD_outer` and `reduceCoOptDir_outer` hold for every solver. Optimality of the volume is the solver's property and is not claimed.
